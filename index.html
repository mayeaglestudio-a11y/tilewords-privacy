Privacy Policy

Good ol' Tile Words · by May Eagle Studio · effective 9 October 2026

The short version.

  • You can play without an account. Your name, avatar, games and history stay on your phone.
  • When you look up a word's meaning, that word is sent to a dictionary service.
  • Signing in is optional, and only for the leaderboard. You can use Google Play Games or a Google account.
  • The game shows ads from Google AdMob: a banner on the home screen, a full-screen ad after each game, and short videos you can choose to watch for a free hint. Google may use your phone's advertising ID to show and measure ads. Where the law requires it, you're asked first.
  • There is no analytics, and we don't sell your data.
  • You can delete your account, and everything on the leaderboard with it, from inside the game.

Who we are

Good ol' Tile Words is made by May Eagle Studio. For anything about this policy or your data, email mayeaglestudio@gmail.com.

Playing without an account

The game keeps these on your phone so you can pick up where you left off:

  • the name and avatar you choose
  • your game in progress, your past games and your difficulty setting
  • your settings, such as sound, and whether you've done the tutorial

None of this is sent to us. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its data in your phone's settings, removes it.

If you use Android's backup, Android may copy this data to your own Google account as part of your phone's backup. That backup belongs to you and is handled by Google, not by us.

Looking up words

When you tap a word to see its meaning, the game asks a dictionary service: first the Free Dictionary API, and if it has no entry, Wiktionary, run by the Wikimedia Foundation (their privacy policy). They receive the word and, as with any internet request, your phone's IP address. They are not told who you are, and nothing else about you or your game is sent.

If you sign in

Signing in is only for the leaderboard. You can sign in with Google Play Games or, if you don't use Play Games, with your Google account. Both go through Firebase Authentication, a Google service. The two are separate accounts: if you sign in once with each, you have two leaderboard entries.

With Play Games. Play Games shares your Play Games player ID and gamer name with Firebase. No email address is asked for.

With a Google account. Google shares your email address, your Google name and a link to your profile photo with Firebase, which keeps your email address with your account.

What the game uses. An account ID that Firebase creates, and your gamer name or Google name, which the game shows only to you, as the account you're signed in with. Your email address and photo are never shown in the game, and your in-game name and avatar are never replaced by your account details.

What the leaderboard stores.

For each game you win while signed in your account ID, in-game name and avatar; the difficulty; your scores; which hints you used, and how many were for watching an ad; how the game ended; every move played; and the time. When you first sign in, your best wins from before you signed in on this phone are added too.
Your leaderboard entry your in-game name and avatar, your best scores (all-time and this month), how many games you've played, and when you last played.

Why the moves are stored. Every copy of the game checks each score on the leaderboard by replaying the moves behind it, so scores can't simply be made up.

Who can see it. Other signed-in players see the leaderboard: your in-game name, avatar, scores, number of games and best word. To check scores, their game also downloads the game records behind the leaderboard, which include your account ID and moves. Nobody else ever sees your email address, gamer name, Google name or photo.

Where it's kept. In Google Cloud Firestore, in Mumbai, India. Google runs this for us; see Firebase's privacy information.

How long. Until you delete your account.

Ads

The game is free and is paid for by ads, served by Google AdMob. You'll see a banner on the home screen (never during a game), a full-screen ad after each game, and, only if you tap to watch one, a short video that gives you a hint without losing points.

What Google collects. To choose, show and count ads, and to stop fraud, Google's ad service collects from your phone: its advertising ID, IP address (and so your rough location), device and app details, and how you interact with the ads. This goes to Google, not to us; we only see totals, such as how many ads were shown. Google explains how it uses this in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and its privacy policy.

Your consent. If you're in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, the game asks for your consent through Google's consent form before any personalised ads, and you can change your answer at any time under Settings › Privacy choices. Without consent you still see ads, but they aren't personalised.

Limiting ads tracking. You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone's settings (Settings › Privacy › Ads on most Android phones).

Ad content. The game asks Google for ads suitable for a general audience (rated PG at most).

Your choices

  • Play without signing in. Everything works except the leaderboard.
  • Sign out from the leaderboard screen. Your name, avatar and games on your phone stay.
  • Change your name or avatar in the game. The leaderboard shows the change after your next game.
  • Delete your account from the leaderboard screen. This deletes your account, your leaderboard entry and all your games on the leaderboard, straight away. You can also ask us to do it; see how to delete your account.

Keeping it safe

All connections are encrypted. Firestore's access rules let players change only their own records. We never see or store your Google password: signing in happens with Google.

What we don't do

We don't use analytics, and we don't sell your data or share it for advertising ourselves; the only advertising data is what Google's ad service collects, described under Ads. We keep no backups of the leaderboard, so anything deleted is gone.

Children

Good ol' Tile Words is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data. If you believe a child has signed in, contact us and we will delete the account.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date. If a change affects what we do with data you've already given us, we'll say so in the game.
